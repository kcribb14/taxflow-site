Lost or stolen crypto

When lost keys, hacks, scams and exchange collapses may give a capital loss, the evidence you need, and how to record it in TaxFlow today.

Losing crypto hurts twice if you then pay tax as though you still had it. Australian tax law can recognise some losses of crypto as capital losses, but the bar is evidence, and TaxFlow deliberately does not decide it for you.

Note

whether you can claim a capital loss for lost or stolen crypto depends on your facts and your evidence. This is general information, not tax advice. Talk to a registered tax agent before claiming.

What the ATO's guidance says, in short

  • If you lose your private key or your crypto is stolen, you may be able to claim a capital loss. The loss arises because you have lost the asset, and its amount is broadly the cost base of the crypto lost.
  • You need evidence that you owned the crypto and that it was lost or stolen. The ATO's guidance lists the kinds of evidence it looks for, such as:
    • when you acquired the crypto and what it cost;
    • when you lost the private key or the crypto was taken;
    • the wallet address the crypto was in, and that you controlled that wallet;
    • the amount of crypto in the wallet when it was lost;
    • that you cannot get the key or the crypto back (for example, a police report for a theft or scam, or correspondence with the exchange).
  • A key you misplaced but might still find is different from one you can prove is gone forever. Crypto sitting in an address you can no longer reach is not automatically a loss.
  • Exchange collapses and scams can be different again: the crypto may become a claim against an administrator rather than something you have lost outright. Those cases depend on the facts and the stage of the insolvency.

Read the ATO's current wording on its crypto pages before relying on any of this: start from crypto asset investments on ato.gov.au.

How it shows up in TaxFlow

Lost or stolen crypto usually appears in one of two ways:

  • An outgoing transfer with no matching deposit (a hack or scam sent coins to someone else's address). It sits in Review as an unmatched withdrawal.
  • Coins that never move again (a lost key). Nothing is flagged: Holdings simply still shows them.

TaxFlow has no "lost" transaction type, and the Koinly template's lost tag is listed as unsupported. That is deliberate: the tax result depends on facts only you and your agent can establish.

What to do

  1. Gather the evidence first, as listed above. Without it, there is no claim.
  2. Talk to your agent about whether and when a capital loss arises, and its amount.
  3. For an unmatched withdrawal, leave it open in Review until you have advice. Answering "My own wallet I didn't import" or "Ignore (no tax effect)" would both leave the coins counted as still yours, with no loss recorded, and the open item reminds you the question is unresolved.
  4. If your agent agrees a loss arose, record it as they advise. One approach agents use is a disposal for $0 at the date of loss, which you can enter in Transactions as a gift given with an AUD value of 0, or by choosing "I gave it away" with a value of 0 on the unmatched withdrawal. That produces a capital loss equal to the cost base of the parcels used. Keep a written note explaining it with your evidence.

Recording it as a gift is only a way of getting the right numbers into the calculation. Describe it accurately to your agent and in your records.

Scam payments

If you were tricked into sending crypto to a scammer, report it to the police and keep the report and any correspondence with the exchange. The same evidence rules apply to any loss claim.
