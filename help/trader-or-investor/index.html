Trader or investor?

The difference between holding crypto as an investment and carrying on a crypto trading business, and what changes in TaxFlow when you switch.

Updated 17 October 2026

On this page

  2. What changes in TaxFlow

Most people who buy and sell crypto, even often, are investors: their crypto is a CGT asset, and gains and losses go through the capital gains rules. A smaller group carry on a business of trading crypto. For them, crypto is trading stock: sales are business income, purchases are deductions, and there is no CGT discount. TaxFlow supports both. The choice is set under Treatment in Settings, and it changes every figure.

Note

whether you are in business is a question of fact, and TaxFlow cannot decide it for you. It gives general information, not tax advice. If you are thinking of treating yourself as a trader, get advice from a registered tax agent first.

How the ATO tells them apart

The ATO looks at the whole picture. Indicators that point to a business include:

  • you trade for profit, with a business plan and a strategy, not just hoping prices rise;
  • the volume, frequency and size of your trades are large and repeated;
  • you run it in a businesslike way: records, systems, perhaps an ABN, registered for GST where required;
  • you have relevant knowledge and spend substantial time on it.

Frequent trading on its own does not make you a trader. Someone who buys and holds for long-term growth, or trades in their spare time with their savings, is generally an investor. Being a trader cuts both ways: losses can be revenue losses (subject to the non-commercial loss rules for individuals), but you lose the 50% discount on long-held gains.

What changes in TaxFlow

Investor (default) Trader
Sales Capital gains or losses, item 18 Business income (gross sales)
Purchases Cost base of each parcel Deductible purchases of trading stock
CGT discount 50% after 12 months (individuals) None
Coins held at 30 June Nothing happens Closing stock, valued at cost, market value or the lower of the two
Staking and other income Other income Other business income
Airdrops Not income under draft TR 2026/D1 Business income
Parcel method FIFO, LIFO, HIFO or LTFO FIFO only
Collectable and personal use categories Applied Ignored: everything is stock
Labels H, A, V Your capital gains Zero

Trader mode is not available for an SMSF (TaxFlow treats fund crypto as on capital account) or for any year with a foreign or temporary residency period. If you choose a combination that does not work, Settings explains which setting to change, and figures pause until you do.

The trader working

Reports shows Your trading business:

  • Sales of trading stock (gross proceeds, including crypto spent and fees paid in crypto)
  • Other business income (crypto received in the business)
  • Opening stock (last year's closing stock) and Purchases
  • Closing stock
  • Selling expenses and Other expenses (fees paid in crypto)
  • Net business income or loss = total business income, less purchases and expenses, plus closing stock, less opening stock

These figures go in the business and professional items schedule of an individual return, or the company return; your agent will confirm the exact labels. trading.csv in the tax agent pack has the working for every year.

Switching treatment

You can switch to compare, and switch back. TaxFlow recalculates everything. Changing how you are actually treated from one year to the next is a different matter (assets moving from capital account to trading stock can have their own consequences), so talk to your agent rather than flipping the setting per year.
