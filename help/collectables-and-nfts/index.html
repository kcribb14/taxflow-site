Collectables and NFTs

When an NFT may be a collectable, the $500 threshold, why collectable losses can only offset collectable gains, and how to mark them in TaxFlow.

Updated 17 October 2026

Collectables are a CGT category with their own rules: artwork, jewellery, antiques, rare coins and stamps, and similar items kept mainly for personal use or enjoyment. An NFT is a crypto asset, but depending on what it gives you (for example rights to a piece of artwork), it may be a collectable. Most fungible crypto, such as BTC or ETH, is not a collectable.

Note

how an NFT should be classified depends on the rights it carries and why you hold it, and the ATO has not settled every case. This is general information, not tax advice. Ask a registered tax agent before treating NFTs as collectables.

The rules TaxFlow applies

Collectable Other crypto
Asset cost $500 or less Gain or loss disregarded Taxed as normal
Asset cost more than $500 Gain or loss counts Taxed as normal
CGT discount after 12 months Yes Yes
Losses can offset Collectable gains only Any capital gains
Unused losses carry forward Separately, as net collectable losses As net capital losses

The $500 test applies to what you paid for the asset (the first element of its cost base). Items bought together as a set count as one asset.

How collectable losses work

  1. Collectable losses this year, then collectable losses carried in, come off collectable gains (the non-discount ones first).
  2. Any collectable gain left over joins your other gains and goes through the normal method statement: other losses, then the discount.
  3. Unused collectable losses carry forward on their own, and can only be used against future collectable gains.

So label H includes collectable gains after collectable losses. On the CGT schedule, collectable gains and losses have their own rows, and label V splits into collectable losses (3A) and other losses (3B). TaxFlow's CGT schedule panel shows each; whether item 1H should be shown before or after collectable losses is a point the app flags for your agent.

Marking an NFT as a collectable in TaxFlow

  1. Open Transactions and find the purchase (or mint, or receipt) of the NFT.
  2. Open its details and set Category to Collectable.
  3. When you sell, the sale uses collectable parcels first.

If you had collectable losses from before your first imported year, enter them in Settings as Collectable losses carried in (AUD).

NFTs and GST

If you are registered for GST, selling an NFT is generally not a supply of digital currency, so it may be a taxable supply. List NFT asset codes under Assets that are taxable supplies in Settings. See BAS and GST for crypto.

Practical tips

  • Give each NFT its own asset code in your files (for example the collection name and token id), so each is its own asset and its own parcel. If several NFTs share one code, TaxFlow treats them as units of the same asset.
  • Gas paid to mint is part of the NFT's cost base; gas paid to sell reduces your gain.
  • Royalties you receive as a creator are ordinary income, not collectables.
