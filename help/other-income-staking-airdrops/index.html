Other income: staking, rewards and airdrops

Where crypto income goes on your return, how TaxFlow values and groups it, and why most airdrops are not income under the draft ruling.

Updated 17 October 2026

Some crypto you receive is ordinary income: taxed at your marginal rate in the year you receive it, at its AUD market value on the day. It goes in Other income, separate from your capital gains. The same value becomes the cost base of the coins, so when you later sell them you are only taxed on the change in value since you received them.

What TaxFlow counts as income

Type Examples Description TaxFlow uses
Staking Staking rewards from an exchange or validator Crypto staking rewards
Interest Earn and lending interest Crypto interest
Mining Mining rewards Crypto mining income
Reward Referral bonuses, learn-and-earn, campaign rewards Crypto rewards
Airdrop received as income An airdrop you got for doing something (goods or services), or in a crypto business Crypto airdrops received as income
Payment Crypto paid to you for goods or services you supplied Crypto received as payment for goods or services
Other Anything else you classified as income Crypto income (other)

Importers set the type from the exchange's own labels (for example Swyftx reward, Coinbase Staking Income, Binance Simple Earn Flexible Interest). You can set or change it yourself in Transactions, or when you classify an unmatched deposit in Review.

What is not income

  • Most airdrops. Under the ATO's draft ruling TR 2026/D1, an airdrop you simply receive and hold on capital account is not income. Its cost base is its market value when received, or nil if that value is negligible. This is TaxFlow's default. See Airdrops.
  • Initial allocations (the first distribution of a new token with no market yet) and chain splits: not income, nil cost base.
  • Gifts received: not income; cost base is market value.
  • Moving coins between your wallets, staking deposits and unstaking: not income.

Values

Every income item needs its AUD value at the time you received it. Some exports include it (Swyftx, Coinbase in AUD, the Crypto.com App in AUD, the Koinly template's Net Worth in AUD). Others do not (Kraken, Binance, KuCoin), and each reward appears in Review as a missing AUD value, counted as $0 until you fix it. Online price lookup can suggest values for many rewards at once, and you can accept them all from one source.

Where it goes in myTax

Reports lists one line per income type, with the count of receipts and the total.

  1. In myTax: Income, then Any other income, then Add.
  2. Choose Other as the type of payment.
  3. Use TaxFlow's description, for example "Crypto staking rewards (365 receipts)", and the whole-dollar amount.

Add one line per type. On a paper or agent return, the same amounts are other income at item 24, category 4, label V. The ATO's instructions list crypto staking rewards and airdrops under other income; which "type of payment" option suits each is something your agent can confirm.

Special cases

  • Foreign or temporary resident. Crypto income received while you were a foreign or temporary resident is shown separately as "Income not taxed in Australia", treated as foreign-source. Have a registered tax agent confirm this for your facts.
  • Traders. If you run a crypto business, income you receive is business income, shown in your trading working, not here. See Trader or investor.
  • Foreign exchange gains on currencies like US dollars held on an exchange are shown in their own block, to go in Other income as foreign exchange gains.
  • Choosing the earlier view for airdrops. If you or your agent prefer not to rely on the draft ruling, set Airdrops to "Earlier guidance: income" in Settings. Every capital-account airdrop then also counts as income at its market value.

Records to keep

The tax agent pack includes income.csv: one row per receipt with the date, asset, quantity, AUD value, income type, wallet and source file line. Keep it with your records.
