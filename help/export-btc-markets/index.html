Export your BTC Markets history

Download BTC Markets' Transaction History CSV (trades, deposits and withdrawals) and, if you like, the Trade History CSV.

Open the app

BTC Markets has two exports. TaxFlow reads both with its built-in importer, but you usually need only the first.

Export File name starts with What it holds Use it?
Transaction History TransactionReport Every trade, fee, deposit and withdrawal, one row per movement of one currency Yes, always
Trade History AccountTradingReport Trades only (fills), one row per fill Only if you have no Transaction History

Do not add both for the same period: the trades would be counted twice. The Transaction History is the better choice because it also has your deposits and withdrawals, which TaxFlow needs to match transfers to your other wallets.

How to get the Transaction History

  1. Sign in to BTC Markets on the web.
  2. Open Transaction History.
  3. Use Export to CSV. Choose the widest date range offered, from when you opened the account to today.

The Trade History has its own export on the trade history screen. BTC Markets' "Reports for Tax" page also offers tax-year transaction reports and year-end balances; the full Transaction History export is simpler because it covers every year in one go.

Add it to TaxFlow

  1. Open Import and drop the CSV in. TaxFlow recognises it by its header (layout transactions or trades).
  2. Keep the wallet name BTC Markets.
  3. No time zone question: BTC Markets writes times in UTC with a Z, so the zone is in the file.
  4. Select Add file.

What TaxFlow does with it

The Transaction History is a ledger: one order shows up as a negative row in the currency you paid, a positive row in the currency you got, and a separate negative Trading Fee row, all sharing the order's referenceId. TaxFlow puts them back together into one trade.

  • Fees. A fee in the currency you received is netted off it (for a sale for AUD, your proceeds are the AUD credited less the trading fee). A fee in another currency is added to the cost.
  • Partial fills. An order filled at different times becomes one trade per fill time, so each keeps its own date and its own 12-month clock.
  • Deposits and withdrawals become one-sided transfers that TaxFlow tries to pair with your other wallets. AUD deposits and withdrawals have no tax effect and are set aside.
  • Running balance check. BTC Markets writes your balance after every row. If the balances jump in a way the rows do not explain (a missing row, or a file cut short), TaxFlow warns with BALANCE_BREAK.
  • AUD values. AUD markets give the value directly. Crypto-to-crypto markets (such as ETH-BTC) carry no AUD value, so those trades are flagged in Review for a market value.

What it sets aside or does not read

  • Buy Transfer, Sell Transfer, Internal Transfer In and Internal Transfer Out. BTC Markets added these actions in 2025 and 2026 and has not described their effect on holdings. TaxFlow treats them as moves between your own BTC Markets balances and lists them as ignored with the action named, so you can see them. If your holdings in Holdings then disagree with BTC Markets' balance, look at these rows first.
  • Rewards and airdrops credited as fund transfers are listed as unsupported. Add them by hand as income or an airdrop in Transactions.
  • The Trade History's fee in a currency other than the quote has no documented meaning and is listed as unsupported.

Gotchas

  • One account, one wallet name. If you add an older and a newer Transaction History that overlap, the overlapping rows are duplicates. TaxFlow reports duplicate transaction ids; remove the older file.
  • Withdrawals to your own wallet need the other side imported, or answered in Review. See Transfers between your own wallets.
  • BTC Markets is also a price source. If you turn on online price lookup in Settings, BTC Markets' public AUD daily prices are the first source TaxFlow asks.
