Airdrops under draft TR 2026/D1

How TaxFlow treats airdrops under the ATO's draft ruling: when they are income, when they are not, initial allocations, and the setting to change it.

The ATO's view on airdrops changed in 2026. In draft Taxation Ruling TR 2026/D1, issued on 19 August 2026, the Commissioner's preliminary view is that an airdrop you simply receive and hold as an investment is not ordinary income. TaxFlow follows the draft by default, and lets you switch to the earlier view if you or your agent prefer not to rely on a draft.

Note

TR 2026/D1 is a draft. Drafts can change before they are finalised, and the final ruling may differ. This guide describes how TaxFlow applies the draft; it is general information, not tax advice.

The three kinds of airdrop

What happened Treatment under the draft Cost base In TaxFlow
You received tokens for doing something (goods or services you provided), or in a crypto business Ordinary income at market value when received That market value Income, type airdrop
You received tokens passively, holding them on capital account (including hobby receipts) Not income Market value when received, or nil if that value is negligible Airdrop (not income)
An initial allocation: the first distribution of a new token that has never traded Not income Nil Initial airdrop

In each case, when you later sell, swap or spend the tokens, that is a CGT event, and the cost base above is what you subtract from the proceeds. Gas you pay to claim an airdrop is added to its cost base.

Initial allocations and the final ruling

Under the draft, once the ruling is final, it applies to initial allocations only if they happen after the final ruling is issued. Initial allocations before then keep a nil cost base.

TaxFlow has a setting for this in Settings: Date the final airdrop ruling issued. Leave it blank while TR 2026/D1 is still a draft. Once a final ruling issues, enter its date: initial allocations dated after it are then treated like other capital-account airdrops (market value cost base, and an AUD value is needed). Allocations on or before that date keep a nil cost base.

Choosing the treatment

Under Airdrops in Settings:

  • Draft TR 2026/D1: not income (the default). Capital-account airdrops are not income; their cost base is their market value.
  • Earlier guidance: income. Every capital-account airdrop is also counted as ordinary income at its market value, as the ATO's guidance said before the draft. The cost base is the same. Some agents choose this while the ruling is in draft.

Airdrops received for services or in a business are income under either setting. Traders' airdrops are always business income.

How airdrops get into TaxFlow

  • Importers mark airdrops as "Airdrop (not income)" where the export labels them: Binance (airdrop and distribution operations) and the Koinly template's airdrop tag. Kraken often credited airdrops as plain transfers, which appear as import problems to classify.
  • Unmatched deposits in Review can be classified as "It was an airdrop" with its AUD value.
  • Reclassify any received-only transaction in Transactions: Income, Airdrop (not income), Initial airdrop or Gift received.

Values

A capital-account airdrop needs its AUD market value on the day received, because that is its cost base. If it is unknown, Review flags it and its cost base is $0 until you add it. If the tokens really had no market (negligible value), enter 0, which TaxFlow treats as a known value. For a true initial allocation, use Initial airdrop: no value needed.

A worked example

You receive 1,000 XYZ in an airdrop for holding another token. On the day, XYZ trades at $0.40 (value $400). Eight months later you sell all 1,000 for $1,000.

  • Default (draft ruling): no income. Cost base $400. Capital gain $600, no discount (held under 12 months).
  • Earlier guidance: $400 of other income in the year received, plus the same $600 capital gain on sale.

Read the ruling itself on the ATO legal database, and the ATO's page on staking rewards and airdrops.
