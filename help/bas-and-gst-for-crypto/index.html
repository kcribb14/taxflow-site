BAS and GST for crypto

When crypto affects your BAS: input-taxed and GST-free sales, crypto received as payment, exchange location, and how TaxFlow fills each label.

For most people, crypto has nothing to do with GST. If you are not registered for GST, there is no BAS to lodge for your crypto, and you can skip this guide. If you run a business that is registered (or should be), crypto can still affect a few BAS labels, and TaxFlow's BAS screen works them out.

Note

TaxFlow gives general information and calculations, not tax advice. GST on digital currency depends on facts like who you dealt with and where. Have a registered tax agent confirm the treatment before you lodge.

The rules TaxFlow applies

  • Selling digital currency in Australia is an input-taxed financial supply. No GST is charged on it, and you generally cannot claim GST credits for acquiring it.
  • Selling to a non-resident outside Australia can be GST-free. Where you cannot identify who bought from you (as on an exchange's order book), the ATO says the location of the exchange may be used.
  • Crypto you receive as payment for goods or services you supply is consideration for a taxable supply (unless your supply is GST-free or input-taxed for another reason). That is ordinary GST on your sale.
  • NFTs and other tokens that are not digital currency can be taxable supplies. You list which asset codes are taxable in Settings.
  • Buying crypto carries no GST credits, and exchange fees are treated as financial acquisitions without credits.

Turn it on

In Settings, under GST and BAS:

  1. Tick I am registered for GST.
  2. Choose Quarterly or Monthly reporting.
  3. Choose the basis for sales of digital currency: Margin (the default; the BAS counts the gain on input-taxed and GST-free currency sales, never below zero) or Gross (the full sale price, and crypto purchases as non-capital purchases).
  4. Set My prices include GST for your taxable sales.
  5. List any Assets that are taxable supplies (for example NFT codes).
  6. Confirm Where each wallet is: Australia or Overseas.

Exchange location is your call

Do not assume an exchange is overseas from its brand. Several global brands serve Australians through Australian companies: Kraken, Coinbase's Australian retail service, Binance Australia and the Crypto.com App all name Australian entities in their Australian terms, and the entity can differ by product and by date. The BAS screen refuses to show totals until you confirm the location of every wallet from Kraken, Binance, Coinbase, Crypto.com, OKX and KuCoin. Australian exchanges (CoinSpot, Swyftx, BTC Markets and others) default to Australia. Check your account agreement for the period, and if the entity changed part-way, split the files into separate wallets.

How the labels are filled

Label What TaxFlow puts there
G1 Total sales Taxable supplies (crypto payments received, taxable assets sold in Australia) plus the input-taxed and GST-free currency amounts on your chosen basis, plus manual sales
G2 Export sales Your manual export lines only (crypto is never G2)
G3 Other GST-free sales GST-free currency supplies through overseas wallets, plus manual GST-free lines
G4 Input taxed sales Input-taxed currency supplies (worksheet method only)
G10, G11 Purchases Your manual lines; under the gross basis, G11 also includes crypto bought
1A GST on sales One eleventh of taxable sales (or 10% on top if prices exclude GST), plus manual GST
1B GST on purchases Manual lines only

The simpler BAS uses only G1, 1A and 1B; TaxFlow shows both versions. A Worksheet of supplies lists each sale or swap in the period with its class, location, proceeds, margin and GST. Download CSV gives the labels and worksheet.

You can add manual lines for non-crypto sales and purchases, so the BAS is complete for the business.

Registration threshold

TaxFlow shows your GST turnover for the 12 months to the end of the period. If you carry on an enterprise, you must register once your GST turnover reaches $75,000. Input-taxed sales of digital currency do not count towards it.
