Try the sample portfolio

Explore TaxFlow with a made-up Perth investor's CoinSpot, Swyftx and Ledger history before you import your own files.

Follow along in the app. Your files stay on your device.

Open the app

The sample portfolio is a made-up investor called Sam, who lives in Perth and has traded since 2022. It is the fastest way to see what TaxFlow does with real-looking exports, including the things that go wrong. No real person's data is in it.

How to open it

  • From this website, use Try the sample portfolio. It opens the app through Welcome, loads the sample and lands on Overview. If you already have a project of your own, the sample goes into a separate project named "Sample portfolio", so it never overwrites your work.
  • Inside the app, open Import and scroll to Try the sample portfolio, then Load sample portfolio. If your current project already has files, the app asks before replacing it. Export your project first from Settings if you want to keep it.

What is in it

Four synthetic files, each in the exact layout the real exchange writes:

File Source What it shows
coinspot-orders.csv CoinSpot Buys/Sells CSV Bitcoin and Ether bought and sold for AUD
coinspot-sends-receives.csv CoinSpot Sends/Receives CSV Bitcoin sent from CoinSpot to a Ledger
swyftx-transactions.csv Swyftx transaction report Solana trades and Cardano staking rewards
ledger-koinly.csv Koinly universal template Swaps made from the Ledger hardware wallet

The CoinSpot and Swyftx files have no time zone in them, so the sample sets their export zone to Australia/Perth, which is also the project's time zone. The history covers three tax years: 2022-23, 2023-24 and 2024-25.

What to look at

  1. Overview. Pick a tax year at the top. You will see the four return boxes (H, A, V and other income), a waterfall from gains to net capital gain, and how the result would differ under FIFO, LIFO, HIFO and LTFO.
  2. Review. The sample is deliberately not clean. It includes a deposit with no matching withdrawal (so you can try classifying it) and a swap with no AUD value (so you can try entering one). Fix them and watch the figures change. The transfer from CoinSpot to the Ledger, by contrast, is matched automatically.
  3. Transactions. Every event, with the file and line it came from. Filter by year, asset or wallet, or show only those with an issue.
  4. Reports. The myTax guide for the year, the checks, the assumptions, and the downloads. Open disposals.csv to see each parcel matched to each sale.
  5. Holdings. What Sam still holds, the cost of each parcel, and the date each one qualifies for the CGT discount.

Things the sample demonstrates

  • A long-held sale that gets the 50% CGT discount next to a short-term sale that does not.
  • A capital loss, applied against gains in the method statement order.
  • Staking income from Swyftx, shown as other income.
  • A crypto-to-crypto swap with an AUD value, and one without.

When you are done

Delete the sample project, or start a new project for your own files, so the two never mix. See Start with your income or crypto records for the real thing.

Check

the sample's numbers are only for showing how TaxFlow works. They are not a template for your own return.
