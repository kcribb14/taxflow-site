Export your Coinbase history

Generate Coinbase's all-time transaction history CSV, plus legacy Coinbase Pro files if you used Pro, and what TaxFlow reads in them.

Open the app

Written from public documentation. These steps have not yet been checked against a live account, so a menu name may differ. If something has moved, tell us.

Coinbase's transaction history CSV lists every buy, sell, convert, send, receive and reward in your account. TaxFlow reads all five header generations Coinbase has used over the years, and the legacy Coinbase Pro fills and account ledger files.

How to get the transaction history

  1. Sign in to Coinbase on the web.
  2. Open your profile menu and find Statements (on recent layouts: Settings, then Statements; older layouts: Reports, then Transaction history).
  3. Choose Generate a custom statement, with All time, All assets and All transactions.
  4. Choose CSV, not PDF.

Coinbase has moved this page several times, so the labels may differ on your account. What you want is the all-time transaction history as CSV. Other Coinbase reports, such as the gain/loss report and tax forms, are summaries that TaxFlow cannot use.

If you used Coinbase Pro

Coinbase Pro was folded into Advanced Trade. For the years you used Pro, also download its fills and account statements (CSV) from Coinbase Exchange's Statements page, if you still have access. TaxFlow reads both (layouts pro-fills and pro-ledger). Use one of them, not both, for the same period: the fills file is enough for trades, the account file adds deposits and withdrawals.

Add it to TaxFlow

  1. Open Import and drop the CSV in. TaxFlow recognises the layout, for example coinbase-v5.
  2. Keep the wallet name Coinbase.
  3. No time zone question: Coinbase's times are UTC (they end in Z).
  4. Select Add file.

The file starts with a few lines before the table (a title, sometimes disclaimers, and a User line with your name or email and account id). TaxFlow finds the real header below them and blanks everything above it, so your name and email are never stored in your project's results.

What TaxFlow does with each type

Coinbase type What TaxFlow does
Buy, Advanced Trade Buy A purchase. Cost = Total (subtotal plus fees and spread).
Sell, Advanced Trade Sell, Retail Simple Dust A disposal. Proceeds = subtotal less fees.
Convert A swap. The coin you received is read from the Notes (for example "Converted 0.5 ETH to 0.028 BTC").
Send, Receive, Deposit, Withdrawal One-sided transfers, paired with your other wallets
Staking Income, Rewards Income, Inflation Reward, Coinbase Earn, Learning Reward Income
Card Spend A disposal at market value
Donation A gift given
Moves to Coinbase Pro, Exchange, Vault, Savings Ignored: your own balances
Credit, Admin Debit, Asset Migration, DEX rows Listed as unsupported

A Receive whose Notes mention Coinbase Referral, Earn or Rewards is income, not a transfer.

Every Buy and Sell is checked: subtotal, fees and total must add up. A row that does not reconcile is listed as an import problem rather than quietly used.

AUD values

A row's value is used only when its price currency is AUD. If your Coinbase account showed USD prices, TaxFlow does not convert them (it does no currency conversion of exchange data), so those rows are flagged for an AUD value in Review. Online price lookup in Settings can suggest them.

Gotchas

  • Converts carry the spread inside the amount received. There is no separate fee line.
  • Staking rewards on Coinbase can be very frequent and small. They are grouped by type in other income.
  • Coinbase Australia. Australian retail customers contract with an Australian Coinbase company; older and specialist accounts may differ. If you are registered for GST, see BAS and GST for crypto.
  • Sends to your own wallet need that wallet imported or a "My own wallet" answer. See Transfers between your own wallets.
