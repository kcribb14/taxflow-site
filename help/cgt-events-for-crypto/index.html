CGT events for crypto in Australia

Which crypto transactions are CGT events, which are not, how proceeds and cost base are worked out, and how fees are treated.

Updated 17 October 2026

In Australia, crypto held as an investment is a CGT asset. Each time you dispose of some, a CGT event happens (usually CGT event A1, a disposal), and you make a capital gain or a capital loss: the difference between your capital proceeds and your cost base. Those gains and losses for the year go through the method statement to give your net capital gain at item 18. This guide explains what counts as a disposal and how TaxFlow treats each kind of transaction.

Note

this is general information about how TaxFlow applies the ATO's crypto guidance, not tax advice. Unusual arrangements (DeFi, lending, wrapped tokens, business activity) can be treated differently; check with a registered tax agent.

Transactions that are CGT events

You... Proceeds Notes
Sell crypto for AUD The AUD received The most common event
Swap one crypto for another The AUD market value of what you received A swap is a disposal of the first coin and an acquisition of the second. Stablecoins (USDT, USDC) are crypto, so swapping into them counts too.
Spend crypto on goods or services The market value of what you received See also personal use assets
Give crypto away Its market value A gift is a disposal at market value even though you received nothing
Pay a network fee in crypto The fee's AUD value A small disposal of the coins used to pay the fee
Sell or swap foreign currency (such as USD held on an exchange) Treated as foreign exchange gains or losses, not CGT

Transactions that are not CGT events

  • Buying crypto with AUD. You acquire an asset; its cost base starts.
  • Moving crypto between your own wallets. You still own it. Only the network fee is a disposal. See Transfers between your own wallets.
  • Receiving staking or interest rewards. That is ordinary income when received; the value becomes the reward's cost base. See Staking income.
  • Receiving an airdrop held on capital account: not income under the draft ruling, and not a disposal. See Airdrops.
  • Receiving a gift. You acquire the coins at market value.

Working out the gain

Cost base is mainly what you paid for the coins (the first element), plus incidental costs such as brokerage and exchange fees on the purchase or the sale. TaxFlow adds fees like this:

  • Fee taken from what you receive (for example a sale where the exchange keeps part of the AUD): the amount received is already net, so nothing is added.
  • Fee paid in AUD on top: added to the cost base.
  • Fee paid in a different crypto (for example BNB on Binance): added to the cost base, and the fee coins are themselves disposed of in a separate small event.

Proceeds are what you received, or its market value where you did not receive AUD. When an export gives no AUD value, TaxFlow does not assume one: it lists the item in Review and uses a placeholder (no gain, no loss) until you enter it.

Which parcel was sold depends on your parcel method. See Choosing a parcel method.

Timing

The CGT event happens when the disposal happens, and the date is read in your project's time zone (set in Settings). Time zones can move a trade across 30 June: 11:30 pm on 30 June in Perth is 1:30 am on 1 July in Sydney, a different financial year. That is why TaxFlow asks for the time zone of any export that does not state one, and never assumes it.

Then what

  • Gains on parcels held at least 12 months may get the CGT discount.
  • Some assets get special rules: personal use assets and collectables.
  • Losses carry forward if you cannot use them: Carried-forward losses.
  • Where it all goes: myTax item 18.
