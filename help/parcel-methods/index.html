Choosing a parcel method (FIFO, LIFO, HIFO, LTFO)

How FIFO, LIFO, HIFO and LTFO pick which coins you sold, what each does to your tax, and the records the ATO expects if you choose.

Updated 17 October 2026

Follow along in the app. Your files stay on your device.

Open the app

When you buy the same coin several times at different prices and then sell part of it, something has to decide which coins you sold. Each purchase is a separate parcel with its own cost and date. The parcel you sell decides both the gain and whether the 12-month CGT discount applies. TaxFlow offers four methods, set under Parcel method in Settings. Overview compares all four for the selected year, so you can see what is at stake before you choose.

The four methods

Method Sells first Tends to Watch out for
FIFO (first in, first out) Your oldest parcels Use long-held parcels, so more gains get the discount Old parcels are often the cheapest, so gains can be large
LIFO (last in, first out) Your newest parcels Keep old parcels for later Newest parcels are rarely 12 months old, so little discount
HIFO (highest in, first out) The parcels that cost the most Make the smallest gain per sale Ignores the discount: may sell a short-term parcel when a long-term one would leave less tax
LTFO (lowest tax first out) The parcels that add the least tax after the discount Make the least tax on each sale Decided sale by sale; it does not plan across years

How LTFO decides

For each sale, TaxFlow works out the gain per unit each available parcel would produce. A loss counts in full. A gain on a parcel that qualifies for the discount counts at half (two thirds for an SMSF; companies get no discount). It then sells the parcel with the lowest result first. So a parcel at a loss beats one at a gain, and a long-held gain often beats a slightly smaller short-term gain. If the sale price is not known yet (a missing AUD value), LTFO falls back to HIFO order for that sale.

LTFO is TaxFlow's name for this choice, not an ATO term. It is a way of applying the same rule HIFO relies on: that you choose which parcel you disposed of.

What the ATO expects

The ATO lets you identify which parcel of a crypto asset you disposed of, as long as your records support it: when each parcel was acquired, what it cost, and which parcel each sale used. Where you cannot identify the parcels, a first in, first out approach is the usual fallback. In practice:

  • Be consistent. Pick a method and use it for every sale. Switching each year to whatever gives the lowest tax invites questions.
  • Keep the record of your choice. The tax agent pack states the method in summary.csv and lists the parcel used for every sale in disposals.csv, with acquisition dates and costs. Keep it for five years.
  • Records must exist. HIFO and LTFO rely on your having complete records of every purchase. If your history has gaps (sales with no purchase on file), fix those first.
  • When in doubt, FIFO. It is the default and the easiest to explain. If you want to use HIFO or LTFO, talk it through with a registered tax agent, who can tell you whether your records are strong enough. TaxFlow gives general information, not tax advice.

Parcel scope

Separate from the method is Parcels in Settings: one pool for all wallets (the default) or a pool per wallet. Per wallet means a sale on Binance can only use parcels held on Binance; transfers move the oldest parcels across. Per-wallet is closer to how coins physically move, but needs every wallet imported. See Transfers between your own wallets.

Rules that apply whatever the method

  • Traders must use FIFO: trading stock is valued on a cost basis that TaxFlow works out first in, first out. See Trader or investor.
  • Foreign currency (US dollars held on an exchange, for example) is always FIFO.
  • Categories first. A sale marked as a collectable or personal use uses parcels of that category first, then the method decides within each group.
  • No wash sales. Selling at a loss and buying straight back to create a loss on paper is something the ATO treats as tax avoidance. TaxFlow never suggests it.
