MetaMask and other on-chain wallets

There is no file export in MetaMask. Use a block explorer CSV with the column mapper, or the Koinly template, and expect to value swaps and gas.

Written from public documentation. These steps have not yet been checked against a live account, so a menu name may differ. If something has moved, tell us.

Software wallets such as MetaMask, Phantom, Rabby or Trust Wallet do not hold your history; the blockchain does. MetaMask has no CSV export of its own. To get an on-chain wallet into TaxFlow, you export each address's history from a block explorer, then bring it in with the column mapper or by filling in the Koinly template.

This is the most hands-on part of crypto tax. If you only moved coins from an exchange into a wallet and back, it is simple. If you used DeFi heavily, budget real time and consider asking an agent who works with DeFi.

Step 1: export from a block explorer

For Ethereum and EVM chains (Etherscan, and its equivalents for Polygon, BNB Chain, Arbitrum and others):

  1. Open the explorer and search your wallet address.
  2. Use Download Data (CSV Export) under the transactions list.
  3. Export each type you used: Transactions (ETH in and out, and gas), Internal Transactions (ETH returned by contracts), ERC-20 Token Transfers, and NFT transfers if any.
  4. Explorers cap each export (Etherscan exports the earliest 5,000 records in the range), so split a busy address into date ranges.

For Solana, Solscan exports transfers and balance changes from the account page, limited to the latest 1,000 records per export. Phantom has no documented export.

Step 2: decide your route

Simple history (transfers in and out, a few swaps): map the explorer files with the column mapper. Use the Ledger shape for token transfer files, with the transaction hash as the group id so that the two sides of a swap (token out, token in) land in one trade. Map gas as a fee in the native coin.

Complex history (DeFi, liquidity pools, bridges): build a Koinly universal CSV (one row per economic event) in a spreadsheet, using the explorer data as your source. See Ledger and other hardware wallets for the template and tags. This takes longer, but each row means exactly what you decided it means.

What to watch for

  • Direction needs your address. Token transfer files show From and To. A row where To is your address is incoming; From is outgoing. Filter by your address when you build the file.
  • Gas is a disposal. Every transaction you sent paid a network fee in the chain's coin, even failed ones. Each fee is a small CGT event and needs an AUD value. Approvals with no value moved are fee-only events.
  • Explorer values are in US dollars, not AUD. Columns like CurrentValue or Historical $Price are USD and some are today's value, not the value at the time. TaxFlow does not use them. Leave value_aud unmapped and value swaps in Review, with online price lookup or a price file.
  • Wrapping. ETH to WETH and back is a swap in the data. Whether it is a CGT event is a question for your agent; TaxFlow treats a mapped trade as a trade.
  • Spam tokens. Unsolicited tokens sent to your address can be mapped to ignore with a filter, or left out of your file.
  • Bridges and liquidity pools often look like a swap into a receipt or wrapped token. The ATO's general view is that swapping one crypto asset for a different one is a CGT event, but DeFi arrangements vary. Get advice if large amounts are involved.

Match your transfers

Name each address as its own wallet (for example MetaMask main) and map the transaction hash. Exchange withdrawals to the address then pair with the deposits. Leftovers appear in Review, where you can say they were your own wallet, income, a gift and so on. See Transfers between your own wallets.
