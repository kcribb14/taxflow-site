The 12-month CGT discount

Who gets the CGT discount, how the 12 months are counted (exactly a year is not enough), and the rules for SMSFs, companies and non-residents.

If you hold a crypto asset for at least 12 months before you dispose of it, an individual can reduce the capital gain by 50%. This is the CGT discount, and for long-term holders it is the biggest single factor in the tax on crypto. It is also widely misunderstood.

Who gets it, and how much

Taxpayer Discount Notes
Individual (Australian resident) 50%
Trust 50% TaxFlow does not model trusts
Complying super fund (SMSF) One third (33⅓%) Not 50%
Company None Companies never get the CGT discount
Trader (crypto as trading stock) None Trading stock is outside CGT
Foreign or temporary resident Reduced or none See below

How the 12 months are counted

The asset must be held for at least 12 months, not counting the day you acquired it and the day you disposed of it. In practice: the first day a disposal qualifies is the day after the one-year anniversary of the purchase.

Bought First day a sale qualifies
15 March 2024 16 March 2025
30 June 2024 1 July 2025
28 February 2023 1 March 2024
29 February 2024 1 March 2025

So selling on the anniversary itself (15 March 2025 for a 15 March 2024 purchase) is one day too early. TaxFlow applies exactly this rule, using dates in your project's time zone. Holdings shows each parcel's "discount from" date, or "Discount eligible now".

Things that do and do not reset the clock

  • Moving coins between your own wallets does not reset it. The original acquisition date travels with the parcel.
  • Swapping to another coin does reset it: the new coin is a new asset acquired on the swap date, even if you swap back later.
  • Staking rewards and airdrops start their own clock on the day you receive them.
  • Wrapping or bridging may be a swap; ask your agent.

How the discount is applied

The discount is applied last, after losses. Current-year losses and carried-forward losses are taken off non-discount gains first, then off discount gains, and only what remains of the discount gains is halved. You cannot choose to put losses against discount gains to keep more of the discount. See myTax item 18 for a worked example.

Your parcel method decides which parcels are sold, and so whether a sale qualifies. FIFO tends to sell older (often qualifying) parcels first; LTFO weighs the discount explicitly. See Choosing a parcel method.

Foreign and temporary residents

From 8 May 2012 the discount is not available for periods when you were a foreign or temporary resident. If you were an Australian resident for part of the time you held an asset, the discount is reduced in proportion to the days you were a resident: TaxFlow works it out as half of (resident days divided by total days held). Days before 9 May 2012 count as resident days. See Foreign and temporary residents.

What is changing from 1 July 2027

Legislated changes replace the 50% discount for individuals with cost-base indexation and a minimum tax on capital gains, for gains that build up after 1 July 2027. Companies and super funds are not affected. The detailed apportionment rules and ATO guidance are not final, so TaxFlow does not apply them yet. What is certain is that each parcel you hold on 1 July 2027 will need an AUD market value on that day; Holdings lists them. See the ATO's new legislation page.

Note

this is general information, not tax advice. Timing a sale around the 12-month date can make a large difference; if a lot is at stake, speak to a registered tax agent.
