Personal use assets: a narrow exemption

When crypto can be a personal use asset, why most crypto never is, the $10,000 threshold, and how to mark a purchase in TaxFlow.

A popular myth says crypto spending under $10,000 is tax free. It is not that simple. A capital gain on a personal use asset is disregarded if the asset cost $10,000 or less, but crypto only rarely qualifies as a personal use asset, and the ATO looks closely at claims that it does.

Note

this is general information, not tax advice. If you intend to rely on this exemption for anything material, get a registered tax agent's view first.

What the ATO means by personal use

Crypto can be a personal use asset if it is kept or used mainly to buy items for personal use or consumption. What matters is how you acquired, kept and used it, judged on the facts. In the ATO's guidance:

  • Crypto bought and used within a short time to buy something for yourself (for example, buying some crypto to pay for concert tickets the same day) may be a personal use asset.
  • Crypto that is held for some time, traded, or held as an investment or to make a profit is not a personal use asset, even if you later spend it on something personal.
  • The longer you hold crypto, the less likely it is to be personal use.
  • Crypto used in a business, or to make money (staking, lending), is not personal use.

For most people's holdings, the answer is no.

How the exemption works

Cost of the crypto (first element of cost base) Capital gain Capital loss
$10,000 or less Disregarded Disregarded
More than $10,000 Taxed as normal Disregarded

A loss on a personal use asset is always disregarded, whatever the cost. The $10,000 test applies to what you paid for the asset (not to the value when spent), and a set of items bought together counts as one asset.

Marking crypto as personal use in TaxFlow

TaxFlow never applies this exemption on its own. You mark it per transaction:

  1. Open Transactions and find the purchase of the crypto you bought for personal use.
  2. Open its details and set Category to Personal use.
  3. If the spend is a separate transaction, set its category to Personal use too, so it uses the personal use parcels first.

The category goes on the parcels the purchase creates. A disposal marked personal use takes parcels of that category first, then others. TaxFlow tests each parcel's original cost against $10,000.

What you will see

A disregarded disposal still appears in disposals.csv, with the reason, so your records are complete. It is left out of labels H, A and V and of total capital proceeds. Reports shows a Not taxable panel with the count and amounts by reason, and the assumptions mention that the personal use rule was used.

Do not confuse it with

  • Spending crypto in general. Spending is a disposal. Unless the crypto is truly a personal use asset, you have a gain or loss as for a sale, at the market value of what you bought.
  • Collectables. NFTs of artwork and similar have their own rules and a $500 threshold. See Collectables and NFTs.
  • Gifts. Giving crypto away is a disposal at market value, not personal use.
