Ledger and other hardware wallets

Bring a Ledger, Trezor or other hardware wallet's history into TaxFlow using the Koinly universal CSV template, then match its transfers.

Written from public documentation. These steps have not yet been checked against a live account, so a menu name may differ. If something has moved, tell us.

A hardware wallet is just a set of on-chain addresses you control. Ledger Live, Trezor Suite and similar apps can export an operations history, but in layouts that vary by app version and coin. The reliable route into TaxFlow is to put the wallet's history into the Koinly universal CSV template, a simple public layout that TaxFlow reads with a built-in importer. The sample portfolio's Ledger file uses exactly this route.

Step 1: get the wallet's history

  • Ledger Live: open Accounts, select the account, and look for Export operations (or Settings, then Export operations, depending on version). Save the CSV.
  • Trezor Suite: open the account's Transactions and use the export option (CSV).
  • Any wallet: you can also use a block explorer's CSV export for each address. See MetaMask and on-chain wallets.

These menus change between app versions; look for "export" in the account view.

Step 2: put it in the Koinly template

Create a CSV whose first row is these column names, in this order: Date, Sent Amount, Sent Currency, Received Amount, Received Currency, Fee Amount, Fee Currency, Net Worth Amount, Net Worth Currency, Tag, Description, TxHash. Only the date, amounts and currencies are required. The older version of the template, with Label instead of Tag, is also read.

Rules TaxFlow applies, from Koinly's own template documentation:

  • Date is YYYY-MM-DD HH:mm:ss and must be UTC. Ledger Live and explorers usually give UTC; if yours gives local time, convert it first.
  • Amounts are gross: the fee is not taken out. A fee in the received currency is deducted by TaxFlow; any other fee is on top of the sent amount.
  • One row, one transaction. Received only is a deposit, sent only a withdrawal, both a trade or swap.
  • Net Worth Amount with Net Worth Currency AUD becomes the AUD value. Another currency is not converted and will be flagged.
  • TxHash is the on-chain transaction hash. Fill it in: it is what pairs a withdrawal from an exchange with the deposit into your wallet.
Tag Meaning in TaxFlow
(blank) on a deposit or withdrawal A transfer, paired with your other wallets
(blank), swap, liquidity in, liquidity out on a trade A trade (each is a CGT event)
reward, mining, lending interest, other income, salary Income of that type
airdrop An airdrop, not income; cost base its net worth (draft TR 2026/D1)
fork A chain split: nil cost base
cost A fee: the units are disposed of
gift, donation A gift given, at net worth
stake, unstake Ignored
lost, loans, margin, futures, cashback Listed as unsupported for you to decide

Step 3: import and match

  1. Open Import, drop the CSV in, and check it is recognised as Koinly universal CSV.
  2. Name the wallet after the device and account, for example Ledger BTC.
  3. Add the file. There is no time zone question: the template is UTC by definition.
  4. In Review, the exchange withdrawals that sent coins to this wallet should now be matched. Any left over are listed with the nearest candidate and why it did not match.

Gotchas

  • Network fees for sends from the wallet are disposals of tiny amounts of crypto and need an AUD value each. Put them in Fee Amount with the fee currency; online price lookup can value them.
  • Do not include transfers to yourself as trades. A send from the Ledger to an exchange is a withdrawal row (sent only), not a sale.
  • Staking from a hardware wallet (for example via Ledger Live) pays rewards on chain. Tag them reward with their AUD value at receipt.
  • Many addresses, one wallet. Bitcoin wallets use many addresses. Ledger Live's account export already combines them; explorer exports may not.
