What your SMSF auditor needs

The evidence an SMSF auditor typically asks for on crypto, and which TaxFlow files and exchange records answer each question.

Updated 17 October 2026

On this page

  2. A checklist to send with the files

Follow along in the app. Your files stay on your device.

Every SMSF is audited every year by an approved SMSF auditor. Crypto draws more questions than listed shares because there is no share registry: the auditor has to be satisfied that the coins exist, belong to the fund, are valued correctly and were dealt with properly. TaxFlow cannot give that assurance, but it can put the supporting records in order. Ask your auditor early what they want; requirements differ between firms.

The questions, and what answers them

Does the fund own these coins?

  • Account statements from each exchange in the fund's name, showing the fund (or the trustee as trustee for the fund) as the account holder. Download them from the exchange; TaxFlow does not produce these.
  • For self-custody wallets, evidence the fund controls the address: for example a signed message from the address, or a small test transaction to a known fund account, plus a record of who holds the keys. Ask your auditor which they accept.
  • Separation from members. The fund's project should contain only the fund's accounts. Transfers between a fund wallet and a member's personal wallet are a red flag; TaxFlow lists unmatched transfers in Review so nothing like that is hidden.

Do the holdings at 30 June reconcile?

  • holdings.csv from the tax agent pack: quantity and cost base per coin (and per wallet if Parcels is set to a pool per wallet in Settings).
  • The exchange's own balance report at 30 June, to compare against it. Differences usually come from a missing export, staking rewards not in the file, or rounding.
  • rounding-residual-bgl.csv or rounding-residual-class.csv: what the accounting software's decimal limits dropped.

Are the 30 June values right?

The fund must value its assets at market value. Enter each coin's AUD price on 30 June in Holdings. Keep the source and the time of each price: when you accept an online price, TaxFlow records the provider and the date fetched, and bgl-prices.csv carries the source and fetched-at time beside each price. Auditors increasingly want evidence of the market value used rather than a single exchange's quote, so a reputable price source with a timestamp matters.

Were the transactions recorded correctly?

  • disposals.csv: every disposal, parcel by parcel, with dates, proceeds, cost base, gain and whether the one-third discount applied, each traced to the source file and line.
  • income.csv: staking and interest, valued on the day received.
  • records.csv: every transaction across all years, with where each AUD value came from (the export, your own entry, or still missing).
  • The original exchange CSVs. Keep them with the pack: the source references in the files point to their line numbers.

Are the tax figures supported?

summary.csv holds the capital gains figures, CGT schedule items, the checks and the assumptions (method, time zone, discount fraction). The assumptions matter to an auditor: a change of parcel method between years, for example, will be asked about.

A checklist to send with the files

  • Exchange account statements in the fund's name, and 30 June balance reports.
  • Wallet ownership evidence for each self-custody address.
  • The tax agent pack for the year.
  • The SMSF files you gave the administrator (BGL or Class) and their notes.
  • 30 June prices with their sources.
  • A short note on anything accepted as a $0 cost base, and why.

Note

TaxFlow gives general information, not audit, tax or super advice. Your auditor decides what evidence is sufficient. Super law rules (the investment strategy covering crypto, the sole purpose test, in-house asset and related-party rules) are for the trustees and their advisers.
