Staking income

Staking rewards are ordinary income when you receive them, valued in AUD on the day. How TaxFlow imports, values and reports them.

Updated 17 October 2026

  5. Special cases

Follow along in the app. Your files stay on your device.

Open the app

When you stake crypto and receive rewards, the ATO's view is that the rewards are ordinary income at the time you receive them (or the time you have control of them), equal to their AUD market value then. You pay tax on that at your marginal rate, in the year you receive the rewards. The same value becomes the rewards' cost base, so when you later sell them, only the change in value since you received them is a capital gain or loss.

Note

this is general information, not tax advice. Liquid staking, restaking and staking through DeFi protocols can involve swaps and other events with their own consequences. Ask a registered tax agent if you use those.

What is and is not income

  • Rewards paid to you by an exchange (Swyftx, Coinbase, Kraken, Binance Earn and others) or by a validator: income.
  • Interest on crypto lent through an exchange's earn product: income (interest).
  • Locking coins into staking and unlocking them, on the same exchange: not a disposal and not income. Importers ignore these moves between your own balances.
  • Receiving back the coins you staked: not income.

How TaxFlow handles staking

  • Importers recognise staking rewards from Swyftx (reward), Kraken (staking and earn rewards), Coinbase (Staking Income, Rewards Income), Binance (staking and Simple Earn rows), Crypto.com (staking rewards) and the Koinly template (reward).
  • Value: where the export gives an AUD value (Swyftx, Coinbase in AUD, Crypto.com in AUD, Koinly template with AUD net worth), it is used. Where it does not (Kraken, Binance), each reward needs a value and appears in Review. With Look up missing prices online on in Settings, TaxFlow suggests a daily price per reward and you can accept them in bulk from one source.
  • Report: Reports groups rewards by type, for example "Crypto staking rewards (365 receipts)", with the total to enter in myTax under Other income. See Other income.
  • Each reward is its own parcel, acquired on the day received at its value. Daily rewards create many small parcels, and the 12-month discount clock for each starts on its own date.

Valuing many small rewards

Some exchanges pay daily, so a year of staking can mean hundreds of rewards. Each needs a value on its own day. Practical options:

  • Accept online price suggestions, which use a daily price per coin.
  • Bring a price file on Import with columns date,asset,price_aud for each day, from a source you trust.
  • Enter values by hand only for the few that remain.

Use one consistent, reputable source and keep a note of it: the ATO expects the AUD value at the time of each transaction to be in your records.

Where it goes on your return

  1. In myTax: Income, then Any other income, then Add.
  2. Type of payment: Other.
  3. Description: TaxFlow's, for example "Crypto staking rewards (365 receipts)". Amount: the whole-dollar total.

Special cases

  • SMSF: staking income is the fund's income, and BGL and Class exports write it as an income entry plus the units acquired. See SMSF overview.
  • Traders: rewards are business income in the trading working.
  • Foreign residents: rewards received while a foreign or temporary resident are shown separately and treated as foreign-source.
