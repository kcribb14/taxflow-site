Record keeping for crypto

The records the ATO expects for each crypto transaction, how long to keep them, and which TaxFlow files cover each one.

The ATO expects you to keep records for every crypto transaction, and it already receives data from Australian exchanges about their customers' transactions, balances and linked bank accounts. Good records are what let you support your figures if the ATO asks, and they are what TaxFlow works from in the first place.

Note

this is general information about the ATO's record-keeping guidance and how TaxFlow helps, not tax advice. See the ATO's page on keeping crypto records for its current list.

What to keep for each transaction

The ATO's guidance asks for records like these:

Record Where TaxFlow helps
Receipts of purchase or transfer of the crypto Your original exchange exports, kept with the project
The date of the transaction records.csv (local date and UTC time)
What the transaction was for, and who the other party was (a wallet address is enough) records.csv (kind, wallets, counterparty or reference)
Exchange records Your original exchange exports
The value in Australian dollars at the time records.csv (AUD value, and whether it came from the export, your own entry, or is still missing)
Agent, accountant and legal costs Not in TaxFlow: keep the invoices
Digital wallet records and keys Not in TaxFlow: keep them secure, separately
Software costs related to your tax affairs Not in TaxFlow: keep the receipts

Records must be in English (or easily converted) and can be electronic.

How long to keep them

Keep records for five years from the later of: when you prepared or obtained them, when you completed the transactions, or the year of the CGT event they relate to. For crypto bought years ago and sold today, that means keeping the purchase records for five years after the sale, not the purchase. Records behind a capital loss you carry forward generally need keeping until five years after the year the loss is used up.

records.csv gives each transaction a Keep until date, five years after the transaction. Treat it as a minimum: a purchase you have not sold yet needs keeping until five years after you sell it.

A routine that works

  • Download full histories regularly. The ATO suggests exporting your history from each exchange often (every three months is a good rhythm), because exchanges close, change formats and drop old data.
  • Keep the original files unedited. Do not open and re-save exports in Excel; store them as downloaded.
  • Keep a project backup with your fixes and settings: see Back up and move your data.
  • Download the tax agent pack for each year you lodge, and keep it with that year's records.
  • Note your decisions. If you accepted a $0 cost base, classified a deposit as a gift, or chose a parcel method, write down why.
  • Record prices you entered by hand, with their source. Accepted online prices keep their provider and date automatically.

Where TaxFlow's audit trail comes from

Every event in TaxFlow carries the file and line it came from, or "manual" if you entered it. disposals.csv and income.csv show that source for every row, so any figure on your return can be traced to a line in an exchange export. Keep the exports together with the pack; the trail only works if the original files still exist.

If the ATO writes to you

The ATO matches exchange data against returns and may write asking you to confirm or explain crypto gains. Your records.csv, disposals.csv and original exports are what you, or your agent, will use to respond. Respond by the date in the letter.
