PAYG instalments and crypto

How crypto counts towards PAYG instalment income for investors, SMSFs and traders, and how TaxFlow fills T1 and T11 on your activity statement.

PAYG instalments are regular payments towards your expected tax for the year, made through your activity statement. The ATO puts you into the system when your last return shows enough business or investment income; your instalment notice tells you whether to use a rate or an amount. If you have not received an instalment notice, you do not need this guide.

Note

this is general information, not tax advice. Your instalment notice, and your agent, decide how you pay.

What counts as instalment income from crypto

Instalment income is your gross ordinary business and investment income for the period, excluding GST. Capital gains are left out, except for super funds. TaxFlow follows that:

Taxpayer What TaxFlow counts for the period
Investor (individual or company) Crypto income received (staking, interest, rewards and so on) at its AUD value, plus gains on foreign currency. Capital gains are not included.
SMSF The investor figure, plus the period's net capital gain: the period's gains less its losses (non-discount gains first), with one third off the remaining discount gains, never below zero. Losses from earlier years are not used.
Trader (a crypto business) The gross proceeds of crypto sold in the period, plus crypto income and foreign currency gains.

Income received while a foreign or temporary resident is left out. The period follows your BAS settings (quarter or month), using your project's time zone for the boundaries.

Set it up

  1. In Settings, under GST and BAS, tick I am registered for GST and set your reporting frequency. The instalment working lives on the BAS screen, which only appears with that box ticked. If you pay instalments but are not registered for GST, you can tick it to see T1 and simply ignore the GST labels; nothing else in your figures changes.
  2. Under PAYG instalments, choose the method from your instalment notice:
    • Rate: enter the instalment rate as a percentage, for example 4.5.
    • Amount: enter the instalment amount from your notice.
  3. Open BAS and pick the period.

What the BAS screen shows

  • T1 Instalment income: the amount worked out above, with a line-by-line breakdown.
  • With the rate method: T2 the rate and T11 the instalment (T1 times T2).
  • With the amount method: the amount you entered, for reference. Varying an amount is your decision, made on the activity statement.

Things to know

  • T1 covers your crypto only. If you also earn other business or investment income (rent, interest, dividends, other business sales), add it to T1 yourself. TaxFlow does not see it.
  • Crypto income is counted when received, valued on the day. A large staking reward in one quarter raises that quarter's instalment.
  • Investors' capital gains are excluded from T1 even though they are taxed at year end. If you expect a big gain, consider setting money aside or varying your instalments with your agent, so the year-end bill is not a surprise. The Tax estimate can help size it.
  • Missing values matter. Income with no AUD value counts as $0 until you fix it in Review, which understates T1.
