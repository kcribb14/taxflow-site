Closing stock for crypto traders

How a crypto trading business values coins held at 30 June (cost, market value or lower of the two), and how to enter the prices TaxFlow needs.

Updated 17 October 2026

If you carry on a crypto trading business, the coins you still hold at the end of 30 June are closing stock. Their value is added to your income for the year, and becomes next year's opening stock, which is subtracted. This stops purchases near year end from creating a loss just because the stock has not been sold yet. This guide applies only with Treatment set to Trader in Settings. See Trader or investor first.

Note

TaxFlow gives general information, not tax advice. The choice of valuation basis, and whether you are a trader at all, are worth confirming with a registered tax agent.

The three valuation bases

The tax law lets you value each item of trading stock at the end of the year at its cost, its market selling value, or its replacement value, and you can choose separately for each item each year. TaxFlow supports cost and market value, and the lower of the two:

Basis What it means in TaxFlow Effect
Cost (default) What you paid for the coins still held, worked out FIFO No price needed; ignores market falls
Market value Quantity held times the AUD price on 30 June Taxes unrealised rises, recognises falls
Lower of cost and market value The smaller of the two, coin by coin Recognises falls but not rises

Set the default under Closing stock valuation in Settings. To use a different basis for one coin in one year, change it on the Holdings screen. TaxFlow keeps those per-year, per-coin choices and lists them in the report's assumptions.

The value at the end of one year is the opening value of the next, so changing a past year's basis also changes the following year.

Entering 30 June prices

Market value and the lower of the two need an AUD price for each coin held at the end of 30 June (in your project's time zone).

  1. Open Holdings and find the market values section.
  2. Enter each coin's AUD unit price for 30 June, or accept online price suggestions if lookup is turned on in Settings. Keep a note of the source.
  3. Any coin still missing a price is valued at cost, and Review lists it under Market values needed ("missing 30 June market value"). The year is marked incomplete until you add it.

Use a reputable AUD price at or near the end of the day on 30 June, and use the same source each year.

How closing stock enters your figures

Net business income = sales + other business income − purchases − selling expenses − other expenses + closing stock − opening stock.

An example. You start the year with no stock, buy $50,000 of coins, and sell some for $40,000 that cost $35,000. At 30 June you hold coins that cost $15,000 and are worth $12,000.

  • At cost, closing stock is $15,000: income = $40,000 − $50,000 + $15,000 = $5,000.
  • At market value or the lower of the two, closing stock is $12,000: income = $2,000.

When the basis is cost, TaxFlow also checks that opening stock plus purchases less the cost of stock sold equals closing stock (TRADER_STOCK_RECONCILES), as a sign that nothing is missing.

Records

trading.csv in the tax agent pack shows opening stock, purchases, sales, closing stock and net income for every year. Keep your 30 June prices and their sources with it.
