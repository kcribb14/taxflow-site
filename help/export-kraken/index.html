Export your Kraken history

Create a Ledgers export (not Trades) as CSV from Kraken's Documents page, with all fields and your full date range.

Updated 17 October 2026Built-in reader

Follow along in the app. Your files stay on your device.

Open the app

Kraken can export two kinds of history, Ledgers and Trades. TaxFlow reads the Ledgers export. It has every trade, plus deposits, withdrawals, staking rewards and fees, which the Trades export leaves out. If you add a Trades file, TaxFlow recognises it and refuses it with a pointer back here.

How to get the Ledgers export

  1. Sign in to Kraken and select your profile icon (top right), then Documents. On Kraken Pro, choose Statements from the profile menu instead.
  2. In Exports, select Create Export.
  3. Choose Ledgers.
  4. Set the start date to before your first deposit and the end date to today. Ledgers need both dates.
  5. Leave every asset included and keep all fields selected. TaxFlow needs at least refid, time, type, asset, amount and fee, and uses txid, subtype and balance too.
  6. Choose CSV and select Generate.
  7. Kraken processes the request, which Kraken says can take from a few minutes to up to a week. It does not email you: check back on the Documents page, then use the download icon.

The download is a ledgers.csv file, sometimes inside a zip. You can drop the zip straight into TaxFlow.

Add it to TaxFlow

  1. Open Import and drop the file in. TaxFlow recognises it by its header (layout ledgers).
  2. Keep the wallet name Kraken.
  3. No time zone question: Kraken's times are UTC.
  4. Select Add file.

What TaxFlow does with it

A Kraken trade is two ledger rows that share a refid: one for the asset that left, one for the asset that arrived. TaxFlow pairs them into one trade. A "Buy Crypto" purchase shows as a spend and a receive row with one refid, and is paired the same way.

Ledger type What TaxFlow does
trade, spend + receive A trade. Against AUD, the AUD side sets the value.
deposit, withdrawal One-sided transfers, paired with your other wallets
staking (credits), earn rewards Staking income, without a value (see below)
transfer with a staking or futures subtype, earn allocations Ignored: moves between your own Kraken balances
margin, rollover, settled, adjustment, plain transfer Listed as unsupported for you to classify
  • Fees. Kraken writes the fee in the same asset as its row. A fee on the asset you received is taken off it; a fee on the asset you gave up is added on top.
  • Asset codes. Kraken's legacy codes are translated: XXBT and XBT become BTC, XXDG becomes DOGE, ZAUD becomes AUD. Staking suffixes such as .S and .M are dropped, so ETH.S pools with ETH.
  • Pending duplicates. Kraken lists some deposits twice, once pending (with an empty txid) and once credited. The pending copy is skipped.

Values you will need to add

Kraken's ledger has no AUD prices. That means:

  • Trades against USD, USDT, BTC or anything other than AUD are flagged as missing AUD values.
  • Staking rewards are flagged too, because income must be valued in AUD on the day you receive it.

Turn on Look up missing prices online in Settings and accept the suggestions in Review, or bring your own price file. Kraken's AUD daily prices are one of the sources TaxFlow can use, for roughly the last two years.

Gotchas

  • Airdrops and forks were sometimes credited by Kraken as a plain transfer. TaxFlow lists them as unsupported import problems; use Add it manually on each in Review to record it as an airdrop. See Airdrops.
  • Kraken in Australia is operated by an Australian company, and different products can sit with different entities. That matters for GST if you are registered: see BAS and GST for crypto.
  • Futures and margin are outside TaxFlow's scope.
