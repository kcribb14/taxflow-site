Transfers between your own wallets

Moving crypto between your own wallets is not a sale. How TaxFlow pairs withdrawals with deposits, and what to do with the ones left over.

Updated 17 October 2026

Follow along in the app. Your files stay on your device.

Open the app

Moving coins from one wallet or exchange you own to another is not a disposal for CGT: you owned the coins before and after. The network fee you pay to move them is a different matter (see below). The challenge is that each export only shows its own side. Your CoinSpot file shows 0.1 BTC leaving; your Ledger file shows 0.0999 BTC arriving. TaxFlow's job is to pair the two, and to ask you about anything it cannot pair.

How TaxFlow pairs them

Every deposit and withdrawal in your imports becomes a one-sided transfer. TaxFlow then pairs a withdrawal with a deposit when all of these hold:

Check Rule
Same asset BTC with BTC, never BTC with WBTC
Different wallets A transfer is between two wallet names
Order The deposit is not earlier than the withdrawal
Time The deposit arrives within 12 hours of the withdrawal
Amount The deposit is no larger than the withdrawal, and at most 20% smaller (room for network fees)

If the transaction hash or reference on both sides is the same, that settles it. Otherwise a pair is matched only when it is the only possible pairing. When several equal transfers went between the same two wallets around the same time, they are paired in time order. Anything ambiguous goes to Review instead, with the candidates listed. AUD deposits and withdrawals are set aside: cash has no CGT effect.

A matched pair becomes one transfer. If less arrived than left and there is no separate fee line, the difference is treated as a network fee.

What happens to the coins

That depends on the Parcels setting in Settings:

  • One pool for all wallets (the default). Your coins of each asset are one pool, whatever wallet they are in. A transfer changes nothing for tax except its fee.
  • A pool per wallet. Each wallet keeps its own parcels. A transfer moves the oldest parcels first, with their original cost and acquisition date. If the sending wallet has too little history to cover the amount, TaxFlow flags it.

Either way, the 12-month clock for the CGT discount keeps running from the original purchase. Moving coins does not reset it.

Network fees

The fee to move coins is paid in crypto, so it is a small disposal of that crypto: proceeds equal to the fee's AUD value, against the cost of those units. TaxFlow records it as a fee disposal and asks for its AUD value if the export does not give one. It is not added to the cost of the coins you moved.

Fixing unmatched transfers in Review

Each unmatched item shows the asset, amount, wallet, time, why it was not matched and the nearest candidate. Your choices:

For a withdrawal (coins left):

  • My own wallet I didn't import. Name the wallet. The coins stay yours. Best when you moved coins to a wallet you have not exported.
  • I sold it, I spent it, I gave it away. It becomes a disposal at the AUD value you enter.
  • Ignore (no tax effect). Recorded, but changes nothing. Use sparingly.

For a deposit (coins arrived):

  • My own wallet I didn't import. The coins came from a wallet of yours that is not in the project. Their cost comes from that wallet's history, which TaxFlow does not have, so a later sale may show as having no purchase on file. Importing that wallet is better.
  • It was income, an airdrop, a gift or a purchase. It becomes that event, at the AUD value you enter (prefilled when the export had one).

Common reasons a pair is missed

  • One side is not imported. By far the most common. Import the other wallet.
  • More than 12 hours apart, for example a slow exchange withdrawal. Resolve the withdrawal as "My own wallet I didn't import" with the receiving wallet's exact name, then resolve the matching deposit as "Ignore (no tax effect)", because the withdrawal's answer already brings the coins in. Do not answer both sides as your own wallet, or the move is counted twice.
  • Different wallet names for the same account. Use one name per account in Import.
  • Fee taken from both sides so the deposit is more than 20% smaller, common for small transfers.
  • Different ticker for the same coin on each side.
