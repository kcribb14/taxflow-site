Foreign and temporary residents

How residency changes crypto tax: CGT event I1 when you leave, the market-value reset when you arrive, reduced discounts, and how to set periods in TaxFlow.

Your residency for tax purposes changes what Australia taxes. TaxFlow models three statuses for individuals (Australian resident, foreign resident and temporary resident) and the changes between them, set as Residency periods in Settings. Residency itself is a legal test about your circumstances, not just where you live; TaxFlow takes the status you enter as given.

Note

residency rules are complex and the consequences of getting them wrong are large. This is general information about how TaxFlow applies them, not tax advice. Have a registered tax agent confirm your status and elections.

The basic rules

  • Australian residents are taxed on capital gains on crypto wherever it is held.
  • Foreign residents are taxed in Australia only on taxable Australian property, which crypto generally is not. So disposals of crypto while a foreign resident are disregarded, gains and losses alike, unless you elected to keep the crypto in the Australian system (below).
  • Temporary residents (generally, people on a temporary visa whose spouse is not an Australian resident) are treated like foreign residents for gains on crypto: those disposals are disregarded too.

Leaving Australia: CGT event I1

When you stop being an Australian resident, CGT event I1 treats you as having sold every crypto parcel at its market value on the day before you leave. The gain or loss falls in your last resident period. You still own the coins afterwards.

You can instead elect to treat your assets as taxable Australian property. Then nothing is deemed sold on departure; instead, the coins stay inside the Australian CGT system and gains are taxed when you actually sell, with the discount reduced for your non-resident days.

In TaxFlow, add a period with status Foreign resident starting on the day you became one, and pick Treat my coins as sold (CGT event I1) or Treat my coins as taxable Australian property. For I1, TaxFlow needs each coin's AUD price on the day before the change: add them in Holdings. Until you do, the coins are counted at their cost (no gain) and Review lists the missing value.

Moving from temporary resident to foreign resident does not trigger I1.

Arriving or returning

When you become an Australian resident (from foreign or temporary status), or move from foreign resident to temporary resident, you are treated as having acquired each crypto parcel at its market value on that day. That market value is your new cost base, and that day is the new acquisition date for the 12-month discount. Parcels held under the taxable Australian property election keep their original cost and date.

In TaxFlow, add a period with status Australian resident (or temporary) from the arrival date, and enter each coin's AUD price on that day in Holdings.

The discount when you were not resident throughout

For parcels taxed while you held them partly as a non-resident, the 50% discount is reduced in proportion: TaxFlow applies half of (resident days divided by total days held). Non-resident days from 9 May 2012 onwards count against you; earlier days count as resident days. A parcel held entirely while a foreign resident gets no discount. See The 12-month CGT discount.

Income while abroad

Staking rewards and other crypto income received while a foreign or temporary resident are shown separately, as "Income not taxed in Australia", and left out of other income, on the basis that they are foreign-source. Have an agent confirm this for your facts.

Limits in TaxFlow

  • Residency periods are for individuals only. SMSFs and companies have their own residency tests and are not modelled.
  • Trader mode cannot be combined with a non-resident period.
  • The tax estimate handles a whole year as resident or foreign, not part years.
  • Each change needs market values for every coin held on that date. TaxFlow lists exactly which ones are missing.
